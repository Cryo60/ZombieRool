# Military ambient decorations

Creative inventory -> Ambience: Decorative Tank, Decorative Aircraft, Decorative Helicopter, Military Truck. Place their anchor on the ground and right click in creative to configure. The anchor is a technical editing handle; the rendered vehicle is larger than one block. Decorations have model-derived collisions and can be selected by aiming at their visible bodies, including beyond the anchor cell. They remain non-combat scenery. They do not spawn mobs, projectiles, damage or real fire blocks.

Tank and truck: Normal / Broken / Burning. Aircraft and helicopter: Normal / Broken / Crashed. World Wars and Modern eras are available for tank, aircraft and truck; the helicopter uses the modern family. Yaw, Pitch and Roll sliders control all rotation axes; Size controls scale. Placement derives yaw from the placer, and old heading values migrate to yaw. Enabled and the shared power settings support redstone/activators, matching other ambient devices. A disabled decoration is hidden. Burning decorations add a bounded visual flame/smoke effect and a quiet spatial fire sound; they cannot ignite the map.

Existing Anti-Aircraft settings now expose 88 mm Flak, Twin 20 mm, ZPU-4 Quad and Close-in Defense. Their firing mode, power, tracking, turn-speed limit and tracer settings remain shared. Muzzle position adapts to the selected model.

## Models and provenance

Source geometry and diffuse colors were read from the user's local T4/T5 installation with OpenAssetTools. The final geometry is converted offline to block-like surface meshes using selected LODs, palette quantization and greedy face merging. Interior voxel faces are omitted. Sources: T-34, T-55, Type 94 truck, GAZ-63 truck, Corsair, MiG-17, Huey and ZPU-4. The modern family includes Cold War vehicles. Existing damaged source models are used for tanks and trucks; aircraft damage is derived from the intact mesh by removing wing/rotor and hull sections. Crashed vehicles use the damaged mesh with a grounded tilted pose. The Huey's source hull omits its separate rotor FX; stationary block-style blades complete the decoration. Twin 20 mm and Close-in Defense are authored block-style models; the ZPU-4 is source-derived. No claim is made that all variants are untouched source assets.

The final 16 surface meshes live in `src/main/resources/assets/zombierool/scenery/vehicles/`. They share the military material atlas. VehicleDecorModels caches meshes by resource ID and clears its cache on resource reload. Render bounds/frustum checks avoid offscreen submissions. Fire has 8 flame quads and 6 crossed smoke cards, with no spawned Particle entities. Normal scenery does not enter the late effect queue. No vehicle entities are added. Source and converted counts are in `tools/vehicle-decor-sources.json`.

Reproduction: run OpenAssetTools Unlinker with `--include-assets xmodel,material --model-format XMODEL_EXPORT -o TEMP/ZONE` on T4 `ber2.ff`, `pel1.ff`, T5 `cuba.ff`, `khe_sanh.ff`; the named TEMP folder is defined by `tools/prepare-vehicle-decor.py`. Then run the script using Python + numpy/Pillow + the repository PyCoD reader. Only the required diffuse images are read directly from IWD archives; no image archive is unpacked. After conversion, `tools/clean-vehicle-decor-work.ps1` validates literal paths and removes only that disposable work folder, leaf by leaf. Game installations remain read only.

## Map maker / Lua

Decorations use the existing `getAmbientDevice(x,y,z)`, `configureAmbientDevice(x,y,z, changes)` and `setAmbientDeviceEnabled(x,y,z, enabled)` APIs and the `OnAmbientDeviceState` event. Fields are nested in `scenery`: `vehicle` (tank/plane/helicopter/truck), `era` (legacy/modern), `variant`, `yaw`, `pitch`, `roll`, `size`. Anti-aircraft adds `scenery.dcaModel` (flak88/light20/quad14/modern). Use the shared `enabled`, `requires_power`, `channel` and block power config as for existing ambient devices. Family/state identifiers are validated and saved with the block entity. Mesh JSON/texture overrides can be supplied by resource packs; these are rendering resources, not ticking entities.

## Selection and collision revision

VehicleDecorPhysics reads compact collision bands derived from final meshes in data/zombierool/scenery/vehicle_collision.json. Shared transformed geometry is cached per loaded world/anchor and indexed by occupied cells. Yaw/pitch/roll, crash tilt and size use the same transform as the renderer. Rotated long strips are subdivided before transforming to avoid enormous axis-aligned boxes. Thin rotor tips are omitted from helicopter collision; the fuselage and landing skids remain solid. No terrain replacement, invisible filler blocks or proxy entities are used. World queries include these colliders beyond the anchor chunk/cell, and walking path evaluation marks occupied cells blocked. Disabled/unpowered vehicles stop rendering and colliding together. Cache invalidates on config/power/removal. Ray tests use bounds then geometry, with cost independent of ray volume. Creative edit/delete requests are server ray-validated; remote origins are accepted only when the visible surface is within reach. Burning flames keep a vertical upward orientation; their emitters follow hull pitch/roll. The fire texture V axis was corrected.

## Player movement safety

Selection/weapon rays retain the detailed transformed model bands. Physical movement uses the projected vehicle footprint filled continuously from its base to a shared roof plane, at least two blocks above the base. This removes wheel/wing/turret gaps and the small ledges that vanilla step-up could climb into. All movement columns on a vehicle share the same top, preventing low overhangs that force swimming/crouching when traversing the hull. Rotation/scale remain part of the footprint transform. Approaching players are stopped by the side; collision does not depend on their current pose.

VehicleDecorPhysics.recover handles a player already embedded after config changes, activation or loading an old position. It first tries their previous free position, then nearby perimeter sides/corners; a full standing volume must pass the real world collision check before teleporting. Recovery clears swimming/fall-flying only for a player actually embedded inside a vehicle, and does not override ordinary water/elytra behavior. No terrain or placed map blocks are changed. Per-player last safe positions use weak keys.

PortableEquipmentTest now exercises real Entity.move side collisions, jump-height attempts, full columns without underbody pockets and recovery from an embedded swimming pose for all four families. VehicleMovementVisualTest uses an actual survival client walking/jumping/crouching against each vehicle and checks that no swim/fall-flying pose or overlap occurs. Run the visual fixture with `--vehicle-movement`.

Movement safety validation: 311 isolated Forge server checks passed, including side movement, normal jump height and recovery from embedded swimming poses for all four vehicle families. Activation invalidates the physics cache again after the block update to discard intermediate neighbor-query snapshots. Native repeated-movement fixture did not finish validation: its activation setup exposed that cache timing issue; do not treat this fixture as a passed client check.

## Decorative cars (2026-10-19)

Eight authored Minecraft-style models are available directly in the Ambience creative category: Sedan (`decor_car`), Compact Car (`decor_car_compact`), Muscle Car (`decor_car_muscle`), Sports Coupe (`decor_car_sport`), Estate Car (`decor_car_estate`), SUV (`decor_car_suv`), Taxi (`decor_car_taxi`), Police Car (`decor_car_police`). Every item has a rendered inventory thumbnail and initializes its own selected model. The shared decoration screen has an eight-entry model picker, plus the existing yaw/pitch/roll, size, activation and power settings.

Every car supports Normal, Broken, Burnt out and Burning. Broken is a damaged model with missing glazing, panels and a buckled hood. Burnt out has its own scorched steel/ash/rust mesh; Burning uses that charred mesh with the existing bounded upward flame/smoke effect. Burnt out has no flames. All states use model-derived selection and shared safe movement collision, including after rotation/scaling.

Lua: existing `configureAmbientDevice(x,y,z,{scenery={vehicle="car",carModel="police",variant="burnt",yaw=90,size=1}})` configures the same saved fields. Valid carModel values: sedan, compact, muscle, sport, estate, suv, taxi, police. Valid car variants: normal, broken, burnt, fire. Invalid model IDs sanitize to sedan. Existing vehicle families and era settings are preserved.

Assets are authored by `tools/build-decor-cars.py`, without game extraction, downloaded tools or temporary exports. The 24 final meshes share the existing material atlas and resource-reload-aware renderer cache. Their JSON payload totals approximately 1 MiB; there are no car entities or per-instance meshes. Rebuild collision profiles with `tools/tests/build-vehicle-collisions.py` after regenerating cars. Car-only client integration fixture: `tools/tests/render-map-atmosphere.py --cars` (optionally append an existing shader-pack filename).

Car validation: 261 real-client checks passed across all eight models and four states, including body selection, solid collision, safe movement envelopes, saved data, inventory assets, model picker and real menu packet save. No source extraction directories were created.
